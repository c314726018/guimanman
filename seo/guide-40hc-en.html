📦 How much fits in a 40HC container? Complete loading guide

A 40HC holds 76.4 m³ — but how much of YOUR cargo fits? Internal dimensions, the math, real utilization numbers and common mistakes. Free 3D loading calculator included.

Short answer: a 40HC high-cube container offers 76.4 m³ of internal volume (12.03 × 2.35 × 2.69 m) with a 26,500 kg payload. Realistically you will fit 55–65 m³ of cargo — depending on how well you plan.

Why can't you use all 76.4 m³?

Three unavoidable losses: ① edge gaps — a 60cm-wide box in a 235cm-wide container leaves 55cm strips that only smaller items can fill; ② stacking limits — crushable goods cap your height usage; ③ handling clearance at the port.

Rule of thumb: naive loading reaches 55–65%; planned loading reaches 75–85%. Going from 65% to 80% on one 40HC = about 11 m³ extra — potentially one full container of freight saved.

Quick manual estimate

Units ≈ per-layer count × layers. Per layer = ⌊length/box L⌋ × ⌊width/box W⌋ (plus rotated orientation), layers = ⌊height/box H⌋. For 60×40×50cm boxes in a 40HC: 20×5 = 100 per layer, 5 layers ≈ 500–550 units.

Real constraints (stack limits, this-way-up items, weight balance, floor-loading) make precise planning a job for a 3D load planner — seconds to run, shows exactly where every box goes.

Common mistakes

① Volume-only thinking — foam cargo hits the volume limit, steel hits the weight limit; ② using inner instead of outer box dimensions; ③ forgetting the 2–5cm container wall tolerance; ④ weight distribution beyond 60/40 can get your container rejected.
