📦 7 practical ways to cut sea freight cost with better loading

Load factor, palletizing strategy, weight balance — 7 container loading tips you can use today, with a free 3D planning tool.

Ocean freight is priced per container. Every 10% of load factor you gain is a tenth of the freight you keep. Seven tips, ordered by how fast they pay off.

1. Plan before you load

Simulate in a 3D load planner first — you'll learn whether a different container type fits, or that this shipment needs two containers. Finding out on loading day is too late.

2. Heavy low, light high

Heavy boxes to the front bottom, light ones on top. Safer, and avoids rejection for weight imbalance.

3. Tile identical boxes

Uniform boxes in a grid pattern yield 10%+ more load factor than mixed stacking.

4. Fill gaps with small items

A good planner tells you exactly which small items fit the leftover strips and tiers.

5. Measure the real outer boxes

"About 60" versus an actual 63 — every centimeter is multiplied across the container. Measure outer dimensions, twice.

6. Watch the weight balance

More than 60/40 front-back or left-right imbalance risks rejection. Check the distribution after planning.

7. Do the pallet math both ways

Pallets protect cargo but eat load factor (air between pallets). Run both scenarios — the difference may exceed the pallet cost.
